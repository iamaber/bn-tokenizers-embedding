## Collecting a harder, fresh benchmark

The following is an implementation proposal based on the inspected sources, not a published benchmark protocol:

1. Download and pin the **resolved canonical URL**, retrieval date, SHA-256, license-policy URL, publisher, title, and version. For a NIST publication, group by the publication DOI, not the PDF URL or HTML mirror. For GSA, pin the repository commit and individual source files. Strip navigation and third-party material before selecting passages.
2. Select distinct answer-bearing paragraphs, generally 80–220 English words, and keep meaningful surrounding conditions. Write independent BN, Banglish, and EN questions from information needs, then independently review answer sufficiency. BN/BL document adaptations are agent-written derivatives; preserve the original English passage and explicitly record that origin. Do not call them naturally collected Bengali text or independent human translations.
3. Keep related but wrong alternatives: different cloud service/deployment models; CD versus money-market account; depositing a check versus withdrawing available funds; startup versus recurring costs; solar versus lunar eclipse; writing shorter sentences versus specifying the actor. Include spelling variants and code-switching in separate query variants without changing numbers, negation, or the requested action.
4. Split at **canonical source-page/publication groups before model evaluation**. One feasible 180-intent plan is 120 development and 60 held-out intents, with 20/10 per domain where source grouping permits. SBA's small number of combined pages means the business split will also separate broad subtopics; report that limitation. Hold out whole NIST publications and GSA files, and check near-duplicate facts across mirrors/pages before accepting source disjointness.
5. All language forms, variants, and supporting passages of an intent must stay together. Reserve both fresh development and test surfaces from new adaptation data. Audit corpus overlap against the existing train/validation/test data; a newly written question alone does not make its answer passage a fresh holdout.
6. Keep every same-split passage in the candidate index, and review candidate relevance rather than assuming only the authoring passage answers the question. Exact answers get grade 2, partial answers grade 1, and nonanswers grade 0. Record reviewer identity and agent origin. Reject questions with no defensible answer or unresolved translation disagreement. Freeze the holdout labels and selection rule before scoring the new models.

This produces a more demanding **agent-authored, source-backed multilingual retrieval benchmark**. It still has one implementation agent's language and relevance judgments, government/educational source bias, and translated-document bias. It cannot support claims of independent human review, natural query distribution, Bangladesh-specific policy coverage, or unseen pretraining content.

## Transfer method: what the papers establish

FOCUS copies overlapping vocabulary embeddings and initializes additional tokens by a sparse weighted combination of those anchors. Weights come from cosine similarities in auxiliary static token embeddings, trained with fastText on target-tokenized text. Sparsemax can retain several useful anchors and suppress low-similarity ones. Its evaluated source model is XLM-R; reported tasks include language modelling, NLI, QA, and NER. This supports an initialization experiment, **not a demonstrated Bengali E5 retrieval gain**. The official implementation is MIT-licensed and includes tokenizer canonicalization that distinguishes word beginnings. [FOCUS paper, sections 2–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.14481v2), [official code](https://github.com/konstantinjdobler/focus), [canonicalization code](https://github.com/konstantinjdobler/focus/blob/main/src/deepfocus/vocab_helper.py).

ZeTT learns a model-specific hypernetwork that composes original-token embeddings into new-token embeddings while training across sampled tokenizers with the base body's objective. Its experiments use XLM-R and decoder models, and the paper trains the hypernetwork for 200,000 steps; the XLM-R run takes about a day on a TPU v3-8. Applying a base-model hypernetwork to a fine-tuned model is supported in the paper, but that does not establish compatibility with a different 384-dimensional E5-small Bert body. Reusing an XLM-R hypernetwork here without matching shapes, base weights, token semantics, and objective would be unjustified. [ZeTT paper, sections 3 and 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07883v2).

## Lowest-complexity implementation to test

Local inspection found that `experiments/retrieval/encoder.py` currently averages original-token embeddings for each native piece in isolation. It requires the pinned Bert body and logs invalid UTF-8-byte and empty-decomposition fallbacks. Both native 32k artifacts have 31,740 learned pieces plus the existing control/byte block. The retrieval environment has PyTorch and NumPy but does not currently have fastText, SciPy, or scikit-learn.

The recommended dependency-free ablation is **FOCUS-inspired sparse context transfer**:

- Preserve the existing decomposition method as the baseline and fallback. Copy controls by their documented semantics, not numeric equality.
- Identify conservative exact overlap anchors with the original vocabulary. Preserve the distinction between a raw SentencePiece token and a decoded surface; stripping a word-boundary marker can merge two different embeddings. Record the chosen equivalence rule and anchor counts rather than silently averaging collisions.
- On a pinned TRAIN-only sample, collect native-token context vectors from a short symmetric window. A fixed, deterministic hashed co-occurrence vector is a small implementation using the existing stack. Log the hash scheme, dimensions, window, sample order, language balance, and token coverage.
- Normalize context vectors, compute cosine similarity between each nonoverlap piece and valid overlap anchors, and apply sparsemax before taking the weighted sum of the old anchor embeddings. Calculate this in chunks to avoid a full vocabulary-by-vocabulary matrix. Preserve decomposition fallbacks for unseen/zero-context pieces and byte-only tokens whose interpretation is not a valid textual overlap.
- Save the initialization table and provenance once per tokenizer. Compare it with decomposition under the **same new training data, seeds, step/compute budget, English retention objective, and fresh evaluation**. Include static-vector building and initialization in end-to-end compute accounting. A tokenizer's reduced parameter count does not establish better latency or quality.

This is an engineering inference from FOCUS's anchor-combination structure, not a reproduction: hashed context counts replace its trained fastText auxiliary space. If an actual FOCUS replication is desired, train token-level fastText vectors and follow its matching/weighting procedure, pin the library and auxiliary-data hashes, and retain the native-tokenizer boundary/byte adaptation as an explicitly documented difference. Installing the whole `deepfocus` dependency stack is unnecessary for implementing the mathematical core; its [published requirements](https://github.com/konstantinjdobler/focus/blob/main/requirements.txt) include numerous packages beyond the project's current environment.

The experiment must be allowed to fail. A measured initialization gain or recovery speedup on development is evidence for continuing; only the frozen fresh holdout can confirm retrieval benefit. The first-round choice of unchanged E5 should remain the operational recommendation until the new quality, English-retention, and measured workload criteria pass.
